Comprehensive research framework for fl-cl project: literature, gaps, methodology, comparisons, and decisions

Critical gaps your project directly addresses
Gap #1 — Most critical
No prior work combines EWC + FedAvg in a live, reproducible IDS testbed

FL-IIDS (Jin et al., 2024) uses incremental FL but relies on example replay + custom loss — not EWC. Surveys confirm EWC has not been empirically evaluated inside a federated IDS pipeline with real network flow data. Your project is the first to do this in an end-to-end reproducible environment (6-VM Proxmox cluster, NFStream live extraction, MLflow tracking).

Supporting: Jin et al. (Future Gen. Comp. Sys., 2024), Hernandez-Ramos et al. (ACM Comp. Surv., 2025), Incremental FL IoT (arxiv 2603.10776)

Gap #2 — Critical
Static attack distribution assumption dominates FL-IDS literature

The vast majority of FL-IDS papers assume static, pre-labelled datasets (CIC-IDS2017, NSL-KDD, UNSW-NB15). Almost none model the reality that new attack classes emerge over time. Your CL framing — where each new attack type is a new task — directly plugs this gap. The 2026 incremental FL IoT survey explicitly states: "very few studies have investigated the IL paradigm within this domain."

Supporting: Survey of FLIDS (ScienceDirect, 2024), Incremental FL IoT (arxiv 2603.10776), Privacy-preserving FL IDS (Springer 2026)

Gap #3 — High
Non-IID traffic heterogeneity across FL clients is underexplored in IDS

Most FL-IDS work assumes IID data across clients. In reality, defender-a and defender-b see different attack mixes (your synthetic traffic generator explicitly simulates this). The FCL survey (ScienceDirect, 2025) identifies non-IID + continual learning as an open challenge. Your 2-client setup with deliberately heterogeneous traffic is a direct experimental contribution to this gap.

Supporting: AFCL (arxiv 2505.12245), FCL survey (ScienceDirect 2025), Communication-efficient FCL (Zhang et al., 2023)

Gap #4 — High
Privacy-utility trade-off in federated IDS is empirically unmeasured

The 2026 Springer FL-IDS survey identifies that DP, HE, and secure aggregation are "well established conceptually" but empirical adoption in IDS is thin. No paper has measured the ε (privacy budget) vs. F1 vs. BWT three-way trade-off in a federated incremental IDS. Your Plan E2 (Opacus DP) and Plan J3 (TenSEAL HE) directly produce this missing empirical curve.

Supporting: Privacy-preserving FL IDS (Springer 2026), DP in FCL (Chen et al., CANDAR 2024)

Gap #5 — Medium
Backward transfer weight (BWT) is rarely used as primary metric in FL-IDS

Most FL-IDS papers report overall accuracy or F1 only. BWT — the canonical CL forgetting metric — is almost never reported in IDS papers. This means you can directly compare against prior work's F1 while adding a forgetting dimension they don't have, making your evaluation richer without requiring them to re-run experiments.

Supporting: FCL survey (ScienceDirect 2025), FL-IIDS (Jin et al., 2024)

Gap #6 — Medium
Cross-dataset generalization of federated IDS models is almost never tested

A 2025 dataset-centric FL-IDS study found that "most evaluations rely on a single dataset, which risks overfitting to site-specific traffic." Training on CIC-IDS2017 and evaluating on USTC-TFC2016 (your Plan I2) fills this gap and directly replicates the cross-environment generalization test that prior work skips.

Supporting: Dataset-centric FL-IDS (PMC 2025), Survey FLIDS (ScienceDirect 2024)

Methodology decisions — what to justify and how
Methodology choice #1
Why EWC over GEM, iCaRL, or PackNet?

This is the most likely reviewer question. Your justification should be threefold:

  • Privacy: GEM and iCaRL require storing raw training samples (episodic memory). In a federated IDS context, raw network flow data is sensitive — retaining it violates the same privacy principle that motivates FL in the first place. EWC stores only the Fisher matrix (parameter-space artifact), not data.
  • Communication efficiency: EWC adds no communication overhead — the FIM is computed locally and never transmitted. Replay methods would require clients to share memory buffers.
  • Regulatory alignment: GDPR and organizational data retention policies may prohibit raw traffic retention even locally. EWC sidesteps this entirely.

Cite: FCL survey (arxiv 2411.13740) §replay methods; Talpur & Gurusamy 2022 §privacy in FCL

Methodology choice #2
Why FedAvg and not FedProx, SCAFFOLD, or FedNova?

FedAvg is the baseline against which all FL papers compare. Your research contribution is on the CL dimension — introducing EWC into the FL pipeline. Starting from FedAvg keeps the FL component simple and isolates your experimental variable (EWC). FedProx, SCAFFOLD, etc. address non-IID drift via different FL mechanisms — testing these is Plan F1 and E3 (future work / comparative studies). Justify FedAvg as: (1) the field standard baseline, (2) necessary to isolate CL contribution.

Cite: Li et al. FedProx (MLSys 2020) as known alternative; Dataset-centric FL-IDS PMC 2025 as benchmark using FedAvg

Methodology choice #3
Why class-incremental and not task-incremental or domain-incremental?

In real IDS deployment, the model does not know which task (attack type) a flow belongs to at inference time — you cannot present a "task ID" at test time. This makes class-incremental learning (CIL) the correct CL setting. Task-incremental (TIL) is easier but unrealistic. Your 5-class setup where new attack classes arrive sequentially is precisely the CIL setting. Document this choice explicitly — FCL survey (ScienceDirect 2025) confirms CIL is harder than TIL and less studied.

Cite: FCL survey ScienceDirect 2025; Masana et al. class-incremental survey 2021

Methodology choice #4
Why synthetic live traffic and not benchmark dataset only?

PCAP benchmark datasets (CIC-IDS2017) are static snapshots. In a continual learning study, temporal ordering of attacks matters — and benchmark datasets don't simulate real network conditions (interface queue, packet timing, concurrent flows). Your NFStream live capture captures real flow statistics as they would appear on a live network segment. This is a significant methodological upgrade over replay-only baselines.

Cite: Dataset-centric FL-IDS (PMC 2025) §limitations of static datasets

Methodology decision needed
How to handle the IID vs. non-IID axis in experiments

Your current setup is non-IID by design (defender-a and defender-b see different attack mixes). But you haven't formalized this. You need to:

  • Quantify the degree of non-IID-ness: compute Dirichlet distribution parameter α that best describes your traffic split between the two clients
  • Run one IID baseline (same traffic mix on both clients) to isolate the non-IID effect
  • Report drift metrics (Plan B3) as the empirical measure of non-IID severity
Complete metrics framework — what to measure, log, and report
Primary CL metrics
Backward Transfer Weight (BWT) + Forward Transfer (FWT)

These are the two canonical CL metrics. They must appear in every table in your paper.

  • BWT = RT,j − Rj,j for j < T. Negative BWT = forgetting. Zero = perfect retention. Your target: BWT ≥ -0.05 after 4 attack tasks.
  • FWT = Ri-1,i − bi where bi is the random baseline. Positive FWT = the model generalized to task i before seeing it.
  • Intransigence = the difficulty of learning new tasks due to over-regularization (EWC λ too high). Reported as Ri,i − R*i where R*i is single-task upper bound.
Primary FL metrics
Communication overhead + convergence speed + client drift
  • Communication bytes per round: gRPC payload size aggregator↔clients (log as MLflow metric)
  • Rounds to convergence: number of FL rounds until global F1 plateaus within ε=0.01
  • Client drift (L2 norm): ||wlocal − wglobal||₂ per client per round (Plan B3)
  • Staleness index: how many rounds before the deployed champion model degrades below threshold F1
Classification metrics (per class)
F1, precision, recall per attack class — not just macro average

Report per-class F1 separately for: Benign, SSH Brute-force, Slowloris, DNS Exfiltration, Botnet. Macro F1 hides poor detection of rare classes. Reviewers in the security domain will look for per-class breakdown.

  • Also report: confusion matrix evolution (Plan I3)
  • False negative rate on attack classes is more security-relevant than overall accuracy
  • False positive rate on benign traffic matters for operational cost (alert fatigue)
Efficiency metrics
Training time, memory, model size — important for edge IDS claims
  • Seconds per local training epoch (log per round)
  • FIM storage cost: size of Fisher matrix per layer in bytes
  • Peak RAM per client during EWC backward pass
  • Model size in bytes (TorchScript export)
  • These matter for the deployment narrative: can this run on a network appliance?
Metrics NOT to use as primary
Accuracy — why it misleads in IDS

Network traffic is heavily imbalanced — benign flows vastly outnumber attack flows. A model that classifies everything as benign gets 95%+ accuracy but 0% attack detection. Use weighted F1 as primary, accuracy only as secondary context. Most prior FL-IDS papers use accuracy as primary metric — this is a known weakness. Flagging it in your paper is a direct critique of the field's evaluation practice.

Key research decisions — what to commit to and why
Commit now
Decision 1: Fix your CL problem setting as class-incremental (CIL)

Do not change this. Class-incremental is harder, more realistic, and less studied than task-incremental. It also makes your comparison table clean: FL-IIDS (Jin 2024) is also CIL, so you can compare directly.

Commit now
Decision 2: Use CIC-IDS2017 PCAP replay as your reproducible benchmark condition

All comparative claims and ablations must be run on this fixed condition, not live synthetic traffic. Live traffic is your novelty demonstration; replay is your reproducible measurement. Both appear in the paper — one as the benchmark table, one as the deployment case study.

Commit now
Decision 3: EWC lambda ablation (Plan D1) is your core experimental result

Every other experiment either motivates D1 (baselines), extends D1 (comparisons), or applies D1's findings (deployment). Structure the paper around this. The Pareto curve of BWT vs. F1 at different λ values is your Figure 1.

Decision 5: How to handle the non-IID axis

Three options: (A) acknowledge it qualitatively and leave quantification as future work; (B) run the IID vs. non-IID baseline comparison (Plan B3 + one extra run); (C) introduce Dirichlet α parameterization and run a full non-IID severity sweep. Option B is the minimum viable contribution. Option C is publishable on its own.

Open question — research finding
Does EWC λ interact with FL round count?

This is an unexplored question: at low λ, does running more FL rounds compensate for forgetting (because the global model "reminds" local models of old tasks via FedAvg)? At high λ, do more FL rounds reduce plasticity further? This interaction effect, if found, is a novel empirical finding not reported in any prior FL-IDS or FCL paper.
